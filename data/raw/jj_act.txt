ACT NAME: JUVENILE JUSTICE (CARE AND PROTECTION OF CHILDREN) ACT 2015 (JJ ACT)
ACT NUMBER: 2 OF 2016
LAST AMENDED: 2021
RELEVANCE: Medical, Child Protection, Reporting Obligations, Hospital Duties, Minor Patients

---

SECTION 2: KEY DEFINITIONS FOR DOCTORS
2(12) "child" means a person who has not completed eighteen years of age.
2(13) "child in conflict with law" means a child alleged or found to have committed an offence and who has not completed eighteen years of age on the date of commission of such offence.
2(14) "child in need of care and protection" means a child who:
- is found without home or means of subsistence
- is found working in contravention of labour laws or begging
- has been injured, exploited, abused or neglected by a guardian
- has been or is being abused or tortured for sexual abuse or illegal acts
- is a victim of armed conflict, civil unrest or natural calamity
- is at imminent risk of underage marriage

DOCTOR RELEVANCE: A child brought to hospital showing signs of abuse, neglect, malnutrition, sexual assault, or exploitation qualifies as a "child in need of care and protection" and must be reported.

---

SECTION 3: GENERAL PRINCIPLES — GUIDES ALL DECISIONS ABOUT CHILDREN
(i) Presumption of innocence: Any child shall be presumed innocent of criminal intent up to 18 years.
(iv) Best interest of child: All decisions shall be based on what is in the best interest of the child.
(vi) Principle of safety: All measures shall be taken to ensure the child is safe and not subjected to harm, abuse or maltreatment.
(xi) Right to privacy and confidentiality: Every child has a right to protection of privacy throughout judicial proceedings.

DOCTOR NOTE: When treating a minor, confidentiality and best interest of the child are mandatory guiding principles.

---

SECTION 31: PRODUCTION BEFORE CHILD WELFARE COMMITTEE — DOCTOR OBLIGATION
Any child in need of care and protection may be produced before the Child Welfare Committee (CWC) by:
(vii) ANY NURSE, DOCTOR OR MANAGEMENT OF A NURSING HOME, HOSPITAL OR MATERNITY HOME.

DOCTOR NOTE: Doctors and hospitals are explicitly listed as persons who CAN and SHOULD produce a child in need of care and protection before the CWC. The child must be produced within 24 hours.

---

SECTION 32: MANDATORY REPORTING — ABANDONED OR LOST CHILD
Any individual or a hospital or maternity home who finds or is handed over a child who appears:
- abandoned or lost, OR
- an orphan without family support

SHALL within TWENTY-FOUR HOURS give information to:
- Childline Services (Dial 1098), OR
- Nearest police station, OR
- Child Welfare Committee, OR
- District Child Protection Unit

OR hand over the child to a registered child care institution.

DOCTOR NOTE: If an unaccompanied child is brought to your hospital with no guardian traceable, you MUST report to one of the above within 24 hours. Failure is a criminal offence.

---

SECTION 33 AND 34: PENALTY FOR NOT REPORTING
Failure to report an abandoned or lost child within 24 hours is a criminal offence.
PENALTY: Imprisonment up to 6 months OR fine of Rs. 10,000 OR both.

---

SECTION 74: PROHIBITION ON DISCLOSURE OF CHILD'S IDENTITY
No report in any media or communication shall disclose the name, address, school or any particulars that may lead to identification of:
- A child in conflict with law
- A child in need of care and protection
- A child victim or witness of a crime

PENALTY for violation: Imprisonment up to 6 months OR fine up to Rs. 2 lakhs OR both.

DOCTOR NOTE: Do NOT share the name, photo, address, or any identifying detail of a child patient with media, police (beyond what is required), or any unauthorised party. This is a criminal offence.

---

SECTION 75: PUNISHMENT FOR CRUELTY TO CHILD
Whoever having charge or control over a child assaults, abandons, abuses, exposes or wilfully neglects the child shall be punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years or fine of Rs. 1 lakh or both.

If committed by a person managing an organisation entrusted with child care: Rigorous imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to Rs. 5 lakhs.

If cruelty causes physical incapacitation, mental illness or risk to life or limb: Rigorous imprisonment 3 to 10 years and fine of Rs. 5 lakhs.

DOCTOR NOTE: A doctor or hospital staff member who neglects or abuses a child patient can be prosecuted under this section.

---

SECTION 81: SALE AND PROCUREMENT OF CHILDREN
Any person who sells or buys a child for any purpose shall be punishable with rigorous imprisonment up to 5 years and fine of Rs. 1 lakh.

Where the offence is committed by a person having actual charge of the child INCLUDING EMPLOYEES OF A HOSPITAL OR NURSING HOME OR MATERNITY HOME: Imprisonment shall not be less than 3 years and may extend to 7 years.

DOCTOR NOTE: Hospital employees are specifically named. Any involvement in sale, trafficking, or illegal transfer of a child is a serious criminal offence with mandatory minimum imprisonment.

---

SECTION 85: OFFENCES COMMITTED ON DISABLED CHILDREN
Whoever commits any offence under this Act on a disabled child, as certified by a medical practitioner, shall be liable to TWICE the penalty provided for the offence.

DOCTOR NOTE: Medical certification of disability directly determines the severity of punishment. Document disability clearly and accurately in the medical record.

---

SECTION 94: AGE DETERMINATION — DOCTOR'S ROLE
Where there is doubt about whether a person is a child or not, age determination shall be sought by:
(i) Date of birth certificate from school or matriculation certificate — first preference
(ii) Birth certificate from corporation, municipal authority or panchayat — second preference
(iii) Ossification test or any other latest medical age determination test — ONLY if (i) and (ii) are unavailable, conducted on orders of Committee or Board within 15 days.

DOCTOR NOTE: Ossification test for age determination is a last resort — not a first resort. Document this clearly. The age recorded by the Board or Committee shall be deemed the true age for legal purposes.

---

KEY MEDICO-LEGAL SUMMARY FOR DOCTORS:

1. Any child showing signs of abuse, neglect, sexual assault, or exploitation must be treated as a "child in need of care and protection."
2. Doctors and hospitals are legally authorised to directly produce such children before the Child Welfare Committee.
3. An unaccompanied, abandoned, or lost child must be reported within 24 hours. Failure is a criminal offence.
4. Never disclose identity of a child patient to media or unauthorised parties. Criminal offence.
5. Hospital staff who sell, traffic, or abuse children face mandatory minimum imprisonment.
6. Ossification test for age is a last resort — use documentary evidence first.
7. Disability certification by a doctor doubles the penalty for offences against that child.
